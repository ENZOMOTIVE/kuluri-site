SLIDE 14

How Hold works

Asked
"Why blockchain? What about privacy?"
Say
"Maria and Nikos accept the terms in the app. We store a fingerprint of the agreement, not their personal data, so both see the same version and any change shows. When needed, they export it for ERGANI or a CAP check."
Data
No crypto for users · no personal data on the chain · ≈ €0 per agreement vs €0.50–2 per e-signature.
SLIDE 15

Challenge 3: data control

Asked
"How does this answer Challenge 3? Who controls the data?"
Say
"Farmers and workers own their records. Nothing is shared without consent, records go with them if they leave, and partners only see anonymised totals."
Data
39% of EU farmers share no data digitally with external partners (Challenge 3 brief).
SLIDE 16

Competition: find · agree · hold

Asked
"Isn't this Agro-U? Or the government's ERGANI app?"
Say
"Agro-U helps farmers find workers and handles the paperwork. ERGANI files short contracts for the state. Kuluri adds the third step: a shared record both sides keep, that can't be quietly changed, for the whole season. We complement ERGANI; we don't replace it."
Data
ERGANI app (July 2026): contracts up to 2 days, no matching. Agro-U: Thessaloniki, founded 2020, matching + hiring paperwork. Farm-work voucher (ergósimo): still a paper voucher paid at the post office.
SLIDE 17

How we estimated the numbers

Asked
"Where do your numbers come from? Are they realistic?"
Say
"Bottom-up, from public data and benchmarks. Our base case is +€78k by year 5. Weighting bad and good scenarios we expect about +€46k, and in 10,000 simulations Kuluri is profitable in year 5 99% of the time."
Data
Adoption: Rogers curve (16% by Y3). First season free, then 70% of farms renew at €25. Co-ops: €5 × 315 members (average Cretan olive co-op, Kotsios & Pappa 2022) = €1,575/yr. First Chicago (bear 30 / base 50 / bull 20) → +€46k. Monte Carlo: 99% profitable in Y5, 68% recover all early losses by Y5. NPV at 40%: +€16k.
SLIDE 18

Costs and risks

Asked
"What does it cost to run? What's your biggest risk?"
Say
"Year 1 costs about €6,500 because the founders build it. The real risk is adoption, so we co-design in the villages and go through cooperatives, who bring their members with them."
Data
Y1 ≈ €6.5k (company + accountant €2.5k, legal + GDPR €1.5k, tech + marketing €2.5k). Y5 costs ≈ €77k, mostly people (3% card fees included).
SLIDE 19

Everyone in the chain wins

Asked
"Who else benefits apart from farmers and workers?"
Say
"Co-ops get full crews on every member farm. Buyers get proof of fair labour for their supply chain, and the administration gets simpler checks. But it starts with Maria and Nikos."
SLIDE 20

Who advertises on Kuluri

Asked
"Who pays for ads? Won't it feel like spam?"
Say
"Ads are a future option, not part of our numbers. If we add them, only harvest businesses: olive mills, farm stores, transport, rooms that sit empty in winter. No loans, no gambling."
Data
€0 from ads in every scenario. The plan stands on season passes and co-op plans alone; ads would be upside.
SLIDE 21

Risk check: bear, base and bull

Asked
"What if farmers don't adopt it as fast as you think?"
Say
"That's our bear case: half the adoption and fewer renewals. We'd lose about €120k over five years, so we'd cut costs early. Weighted across the three cases we still expect +€46k."
Data
Cumulative by Y5: bear −€120k (30%) · base +€78k (50%) · bull +€217k (20%). Bear: adoption × 0.5, 55% renew, 30% of co-ops, costs +15%. Bull: adoption × 1.4, 80% renew, 130% of co-ops.
SLIDE 22

If we win: bigger market, bigger profit

Asked
"What would you do with more money?"
Say
"A grant isn't profit; we'd invest it in the team and in going Greece-wide. Its return shows up afterwards: 2.4× the farms and almost 2× the revenue by year 5."
Data
€500k EU grant over Y2–Y3 (ESF+ social-innovation size, €200k–1M). Crete Y1 → Greece from Y3; co-ops at the Greek average of 183 members (≈200k members / 1,094 co-ops). Lowest point −€24k in Y4, +€91k cumulative by Y5 (vs +€78k), 22k farms vs 9k, Y5 revenue €294k vs €154k.

SLIDE 3

"How does this connect to the CAP?"

Say
"Since 2025, CAP payments depend on respecting labour rules: social conditionality. Today farmers prove it on paper and controls are rare. Kuluri gives them the proof as they work."
Data
Art. 14, Regulation (EU) 2021/2115: voluntary from 2023, mandatory from 2025. Enforcement so far is light (e.g. ~227 farmers sanctioned in Spain in 2024, ~3% cuts). Don't overclaim: it doesn't cover everything, such as minimum wage.
SLIDE 10

"Is €25 a season the right price?"

Say
"It's less than 1% of a small farm's oil income, and one season pass covers every agreement. Below about €17 the model doesn't recover its early losses by year 5, so €25 leaves us a margin."
Data
Cumulative by Y5: €15 → −€4k · €20 → +€37k · €25 → +€78k · €30 → +€119k. 2,880 season passes cover Y5 costs.
SLIDE 10

"What happens when the grant ends?"

Say
"Year 3 is our tight year, before most farms renew. We keep the year-1 surplus as a reserve and are profitable without grants from year 4."
Data
Base cumulative: +13.5 → +8.8 → −19.8 → +0.2 → +77.7 (€k, Y1–Y5).
SLIDE 9

"Why would a co-op pay, if farmers already do?"

Say
"They buy something different. A farmer pays €25 for their own records; the co-op pays €5 per member for the aggregated, anonymised view across all its farms: crews, gaps, labour proof for buyers. Who uses the data, pays."
Data
Average Cretan olive co-op: 315 members → €1,575/yr. First year free. 25 co-ops paying by Y5 → €39k.
